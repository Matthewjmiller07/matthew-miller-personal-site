Running list for the next Workshop. Add builds, links and angles as they happen.

- Mega Bible word lookups (/mega-bible?book=Genesis&chapter=1&cmp=1254,776&concx=1): click any word and you can now show only the verses in the passage with any form of its root (or just that spelling), list every verse in the Tanakh with the same Strong's number, grouped by book and filterable by spelling, and "+ Compare" up to six roots, each marked in its own colour, staying marked as you move from chapter to chapter. With two or more compared, "Together in Tanakh" lists every verse where they all appear (בָּרָא and אֶרֶץ together: 15 verses, from Genesis 1:1 to Isaiah 65:17). Every lookup is in the URL, so a link opens straight onto it.
- The tefillin card off the 3D printer (/3d-print-tools/leshem-yichud#make): the same artwork as true-size SVGs for a laser cutter or vinyl cutter, with ideas sized to a public-library makerspace's machines (Skokie's Studio: Epilog and Glowforge lasers, Cricuts, a Silhouette, two embroidery machines, a heat press). Laser-engraved maple, clear acrylic engraved from behind so the front stays glossy, a leather card for the tefillin bag, a slate plaque, gold iron-on vinyl for a tallis bag, and a one-colour STL that fits the library's free two-hour 3D print. Each idea has a preview rendered from the real card geometry onto wood, acrylic, leather, slate, vinyl, felt and PLA textures.
- The Likutei Peshatim community calendar (/calendars) is current again: it had quietly stopped after Ekev in July, and twelve weeks of HTC's newsletter (119 shiurim, open houses, Yamim Noraim and Sukkos events, and yahrzeits) are now back in the feed. It now refreshes itself in the cloud every Thursday night, and raises an alarm if it ever falls more than two weeks behind.
- Mega Talmud's sugya map (/mega-talmud?tractate=Berakhot&daf=2): press "Map the argument with Jev" on any daf, and TypeSafe's Jev model labels every segment with the move it makes: Mishnah, question, answer, derivation from a verse, baraita, dispute, ruling, story or explanation. It also marks where each new sugya begins. You get a coloured strip of the whole daf, an outline of its sugyot with the flow of each ("Question ×2 → Answer → From a verse → …"), and a filter that picks out every question or every baraita on the page. On Berakhot 2, the Mishnah is laid out as dispute → story → verse, and the Gemara is one long chain of 15 questions and 10 answers.
- The flow of the argument (/mega-talmud#flow): all of Bekhorot, mapped by Jev ahead of time (60 dafim, 1,955 segments, 320 sugyot, about nine cents) and drawn as a woven tapestry. Each column is a daf, each band a segment coloured by its move, and the gaps are where new sugyot begin. Purple Mishnah bands mark each new perek, and the red/green weave is the Gemara's question and answer, which together make up 56% of the masechet. Switch to Seder or Shas to watch the library fill in as more masechtot are mapped. You can pick a palette (including a colour-blind-safe one) or recolour any move, and download it as a PNG. Every daf of Bekhorot now opens with its sugya map already drawn.
- The Bechdel test for Tanakh (/mega-bible?mode=bechdel): does a chapter have two named women who talk to each other about something other than a man? TypeSafe's Jev model answered all three questions for every chapter of Tanakh, for about a nickel. Very few chapters pass: Rachel and Leah over the mandrakes (Genesis 30) and Naomi and Ruth on the road (Ruth 1–3) among them. Deborah and Yael never meet (Judges 4), Sarai and Hagar never speak to each other (Genesis 16), and Miriam isn't named in Exodus 2. Switch on the strict version (mostly not about a man) and Ruth 3, spent planning for Boaz, drops out. Every chapter in Mega Bible now shows its result as a badge.
- All of Shas, mapped (/mega-talmud#flow): Jev has now read every segment of the Bavli, 81,709 of them across 2,684 dafim, for about four dollars. It labelled each with the move it makes in the argument. Every daf in the Mega Talmud opens with its sugya map drawn, and the Flow section shows any masechet, any seder, or all of Shas as one weave. (Tamid's Gemara only starts at 25b, so it starts there too.)
- Shas as art (/shas-art): every masechet, every seder and all of Shas as an 18×24 poster in three colourways, with framed mockups and an "I'd buy this" form that emails me. Questions and answers make up more than half of the Gemara's segments, and on the wall that becomes a red-and-green weave.
- Two trailers (/videos): "The Sugya Map" and "The Bechdel Test, for Tanakh". They're on a new /videos page with Rav Yaakov Kamenetsky's Language of Blessing and the other explainers.
- Mega Siddur's first deep dive (/mega-siddur?n=Ashkenaz&s=Weekday+%3E+Shacharit+%3E+Preparatory+Prayers+%3E+Torah+Blessings&note=47): Rabbi Elyada said only Koren gets the order right, with no verses like רֵאשִׁית חָכְמָה before Birkat HaTorah. I traced it from Seder Rav Amram to ArtScroll through the scans themselves (Heidenheim 1806, Baer 1868, the Siddur HaGra 1900, Seder HaYom 1599, Rinat Yisrael), and the story flips. The "correct" order is the Tur's personal 14th-century reform ("it was difficult for me, saying verses before Birkat HaTorah"). The Gra defended the older order, and Machzor Vitry already opened with מה טבו around 1100. Rinat Yisrael, Chabad and Edot HaMizrach get it right too. ראשית חכמה isn't in any of the classic siddurim checked, including Seder HaYom, where Modeh Ani was born.
- More on /videos: the Workshop issue trailers, the 1 Kings lip-sync reading, the Coffee in Halacha and Trader Joe's spice explainers, the vertical genealogies trailer, and "See You Tomorrow".
- The Painted Torah (/painted-torah): every chapter of the Torah as a painting, 187 of them, with no prompt written by hand. For each chapter Jev reads every verse and chooses the moment to paint. That's a Choice over the chapter's own verses: Genesis 3 gets the cherubim and the fiery sword at Eden's gate, Genesis 1 the great sea monsters. Then it art-directs the moment: setting, light, weather, framing, palette, painting tradition, and Scores for tension and scale. Code turns the answers into a prompt, and FLUX.2 [klein] 4B paints it. All the art direction cost $0.03 of Jev. Filter for the 18 night scenes, or sort by tension (Numbers 16, Korah's rebellion, tops it). Making it took a fight with the image model: it letters whole verses into the picture, so speech is stripped and Tesseract repaints anything with stray writing; and any word for God made it paint a figure in the sky, so divine names are cut from the prompt entirely. There's a one-minute trailer on /videos, cut to a synthesized score. Every painting is filed under its parsha (by the verse it paints, so a chapter that straddles two parshiyot lands in the right one): filter /painted-torah by parsha, see each week's paintings on /parasha, and find the chapter's painting at the top of every Torah chapter in the Mega Bible.
