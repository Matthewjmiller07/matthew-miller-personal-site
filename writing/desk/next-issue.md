Running list for the next Workshop. Add builds, links and angles as they happen.


- **The Ten Plagues, as 3D-printable toys** (`/3d/plagues`): ten snap-together kits, one per plague, built to print on a Bambu A1 mini with no supports. There's a frog that has already split off a baby (Rashi's one frog), a clear hailstone that closes around a flame, and blood on the doorposts as a second colour. The page has a 3D viewer you can pull apart to see how each kit goes together, plus a 3MF plate and STLs for each. Where the commentators disagree, there's a kit for each reading: arov as R' Yehuda's wild animals or R' Nechemia's hornets and mosquitoes (Shemot Rabbah 11:3), and choshech as darkness over the land, or the Torah Temimah's bold suggestion (10:21) that it was a film over the pupil "the thickness of a dinar", which you can feel with your hand. That kit is an eye with a removable film.

- **A toy for every parsha, after Shadal** (`/3d/parsha`): 54 printable snap-together toys, one per parsha, each built on a single peshat of Shmuel David Luzzatto, with the Hebrew of the comment and a Sefaria link beside each model. Some favourites: the rakia as a beaten sky-blue plate holding up the upper waters, with windows the rain comes through (Bereshit 1:6); the burning bush, where the flames stand in the gaps between the thorns and never touch them (Shemot 3:2); the Urim and Tumim as 22 letter tiles you draw from the choshen, alef called "ur" and tav "tam" (Vayikra 8:8); the Levites' open land as a stacking toy, with empty ground inside the wall as well as outside, like Rome's pomerium (Bamidbar 35:4); and Ekev's clothes that "didn't wear out", which Shadal reads with no miracle: God gave them spare clothes and shoes. Same A1 mini stage, 3MF plates and pull-apart viewer as the Ten Plagues.

- **Books** (`/books`): my library online. Every book on the shelf, plus the ones I'm giving away
  and keeping only as a scan. Paste an archive.org or HebrewBooks.org link and it fills in the
  title, author and PDF, with a preview you can read on the page. First one out the door: Areshes Shefoseinu (Los Angeles, 1942).

- **Taking apart a printer** (`/3d/printer-teardown`): I got an old HP Photosmart C6180 all-in-one without a power cord, so I'm taking it apart instead. The page has a 3D model that comes apart one step at a time, in the order you'd do the real teardown, and you can click any part to see what it does. The surprise for me was that there are no stepper motors in it. The carriage and the paper both run on cheap DC motors, and a clear strip and a clear disc printed with fine lines tell the printer exactly where they are. It also explains why an idle printer still uses up ink: it goes into a felt pad.
