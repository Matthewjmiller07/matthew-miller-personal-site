Running list for the next Workshop. Add builds, links and angles as they happen.

- Lost in Translation (/lost-in-translation): a Hebrew mistranslation guessing game. Live cards pick a random verse from Sefaria, run single words through a machine translator with no context, and a judge model picks a genuine misreading, checked against Sefaria's English before the card is served.
- Rav Asher Weiss quote cards: 3 more on 11 October (19 in all).
- The Book of Generations (/genealogies): every name in the genealogies of Bereshit and Noach, Adam to Abram and the seventy nations, as a radial family tree and a lifespan timeline you can scrub by year from Creation, cross-referenced with 75 years of Israeli baby names from the Central Bureau of Statistics (filter by years, sex, and Jewish, Muslim, Christian Arab or Druze). Opening a name pulls its Wikipedia summary and the people on Hebrew Wikipedia who carry it, live. Comes with a 64-second trailer (16:9 and vertical). Best findings: Jubal is the most-given name in these chapters (40,599 Yuvals); Adam tied with Amir as the top Druze boys' name in 2000 and in 2024 was given to 593 Jewish, 664 Muslim and 42 Druze babies; Iscah, mentioned once in the Torah, hit a record 220 girls in 2024, while Nimrod peaked at 128 boys in 1983 and fell to 5.
- Mega Bible now has a Genealogies layer (/mega-bible?book=Genesis&chapter=5): on Genesis 4–5 and 10–11 a card links to The Book of Generations, and every name in the verses is underlined with its lifespan and how many Israeli babies carry it (hover Methuselah: 969 years, 5 babies), with a ↗ that opens that person in the explorer.
- The Book of Generations now covers twelve genealogies, Adam to David (/genealogies): Nahor's children, Keturah, Ishmael, Jacob's sons, Esau, the seventy who went down to Egypt, Exodus 6 and Perez to David in Ruth 4, 287 names in all. Pick one list to zoom the tree in, or see them all. Sort the table in Bible order, A–Z or א–ת. New finding: 115 of the names have never been given to an Israeli baby, among them Cain, Ham, Lot, Terah, Esau and Amalek, while counting the Arabic forms makes Joseph (with Yusuf, 108,000), David (with Da'ud) and Moses (with Musa) the most-given names in the whole Bible list.
- The Uriel Simon Reader (/uriel-simon): a new weekly two-page sheet in memory of Prof. Uriel Simon z"l, the Bar-Ilan Bible scholar who died on 30 September at 97. Page one is a text (the verses, plus Simon's own Hebrew words on them, translated); page two is a teaching and questions for the table. The first three weeks (Noach, Lech Lecha, Vayera) follow his essay on Abraham, "the blessing of contradictions": Noah's righteousness is a finished fact (he "walked" with God, past tense), Abraham's is a task ("walk before Me"); love of the land against pursuit of peace when Abram gives Lot first choice; and the three times Abraham says "Hineni" at the Akedah, to God, to Isaac, and to the angel. Printable PDFs for each week.
- Parasha Hub (/parasha): one page for everything on the site that follows the weekly reading. This week's parasha up top with its Uriel Simon Reader sheet, parasha sheet, picture book and PhDs on the Parsha guest; then the reader series, the tools that work on any parasha, and all 54 parshiyot of 5787 with links to each resource. New sheets and readers show up on it automatically. Linked from Projects and the Jewish Studies menu.
- The Balashon Shabbat Reader (/balashon, and on every week of /parasha): two printable pages a week from David Curwin's Balashon, the Hebrew Language Detective. Each week features the posts whose word is actually in the parasha, with the verse (Hebrew, word highlighted, and English) and the opening of his post in his own words, then the rest of his posts on the parasha and questions for the table. This week, Noach: gopher wood (6:14), the ark's tzohar (6:16) and Mitzrayim (10:6). Pulled from his blog every Sunday.
- Videos on source sheets (/sourcesheets): any source can now carry a YouTube link that embeds right in the card (privacy-friendly player, optional start time), with a short summary and a collapsible transcript underneath. Add it by pasting a link into the source row in Supabase.
- Daf Yomi Arcade — Bekhorot 27 (/daf/bekhorot-27): "Rav Huna's Cellar" — sort six teruma rulings for outside-Israel produce, run Rav Huna bar Rav Yehoshua's perpetual wine-nullification vat (the 2:1 trick from 27a:7), and answer three year-calculation questions on the firstborn's own-year rule from shana b'shana (27b).
- Kolnoa (/kolnoa): a subtitle language lab. A TED talk plays with its human-made Hebrew and English subtitles in sync; hover any Hebrew word and its English counterpart lights up, click words to save them to a word bank. Switch to Drill and the video pauses after each line at your level so you can translate it (Hebrew→English or English→Hebrew), and Jev grades you word by word: which ideas you caught, which you missed, and whether your Hebrew sounds natural. Every word link, difficulty level and "everyday phrase" tag was judged by Jev, TypeSafe's small judgment model; it never writes a word, it only picks and scores.
- Rabbi Dweck on the haftara (/parasha#dweck, and in the Mega Bible): this year The Parasha with Rabbi Dweck is teaching the haftara, and both episodes so far ("Jews Are Blind" on Bereshit, "Beautiful People" on Noach) now have Spotify players on the Parasha Hub and a card on the haftarah in the Mega Bible (/mega-bible?mode=haftarah&parsha=Noach&rites=Sephardi). Every verse he discusses, mapped from the transcripts, gets a note with a ▶ that plays the episode from that moment: Isaiah 54:9, "like the waters of Noah", starts at 6:34.
