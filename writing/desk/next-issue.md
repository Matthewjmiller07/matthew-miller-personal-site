Running list for the next Workshop. Add builds, links and angles as they happen.


- **The Ten Plagues, as 3D-printable toys** (`/3d/plagues`): ten snap-together kits, one per plague, built to print on a Bambu A1 mini with no supports. There's a frog that has already split off a baby (Rashi's one frog), a clear hailstone that closes around a flame, and blood on the doorposts as a second colour. The page has a 3D viewer you can pull apart to see how each kit goes together, plus a 3MF plate and STLs for each. Where the commentators disagree, there's a kit for each reading: arov as R' Yehuda's wild animals or R' Nechemia's hornets and mosquitoes (Shemot Rabbah 11:3), and choshech as darkness over the land, or the Torah Temimah's bold suggestion (10:21) that it was a film over the pupil "the thickness of a dinar", which you can feel with your hand. That kit is an eye with a removable film.

- **A toy for every parsha, after Shadal** (`/3d/parsha`): 54 printable snap-together toys, one per parsha, each built on a single peshat of Shmuel David Luzzatto, with the Hebrew of the comment and a Sefaria link beside each model. Some favourites: the rakia as a beaten sky-blue plate holding up the upper waters, with windows the rain comes through (Bereshit 1:6); the burning bush, where the flames stand in the gaps between the thorns and never touch them (Shemot 3:2); the Urim and Tumim as 22 letter tiles you draw from the choshen, alef called "ur" and tav "tam" (Vayikra 8:8); the Levites' open land as a stacking toy, with empty ground inside the wall as well as outside, like Rome's pomerium (Bamidbar 35:4); and Ekev's clothes that "didn't wear out", which Shadal reads with no miracle: God gave them spare clothes and shoes. Same A1 mini stage, 3MF plates and pull-apart viewer as the Ten Plagues.

- **Books** (`/books`): my library online. Every book on the shelf, plus the ones I'm giving away
  and keeping only as a scan. Paste an archive.org or HebrewBooks.org link and it fills in the
  title, author and PDF, with a preview you can read on the page. First one out the door: Areshes Shefoseinu (Los Angeles, 1942).

- **Taking apart a printer** (`/3d/printer-teardown`): I got an old HP Photosmart C6180 all-in-one without a power cord, so I'm taking it apart instead. The page has a 3D model that comes apart one step at a time, in the order you'd do the real teardown, and you can click any part to see what it does. The surprise for me was that there are no stepper motors in it. The carriage and the paper both run on cheap DC motors, and a clear strip and a clear disc printed with fine lines tell the printer exactly where they are. It also explains why an idle printer still uses up ink: it goes into a felt pad.

- **Tefillin Trials, World 2: the tefillin stand** (`/tefillin-trials`): a new world for this week's SCP chabura (#4, simanim 25–27: which arm, where on the arm, and when you say the bracha). You leave the shul for a stone street corner where you and your kid run a tefillin stand. Each person who walks up has a different arm question: a lefty, someone who uses both hands equally, someone who writes with his left but does everything else with his right, and two soldiers wounded in the war (one lost his left arm, one lost his right hand). Then the camera cuts to a giant arm and you have to set the bayit down on the lower half of the muscle while it slides back and forth. The four zones are the ones from the sheet's picture, and it's scored like the shiur's basketball rule: if any part of the bayit touches the line, it's out. Then you say "lehaniach" while the knot is tightening, after it's on the arm and before it's tight. If you miss that, the game asks what to do, and the answer is to bless anyway, because every moment the tefillin are on is the mitzvah (which is why the bracha is "lehaniach" and not "likshor"). In between come shailos: a bar mitzvah boy whose shel yad slipped mid-davening, Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam worn together (under 8 cm), and talking between the brachot when you put them on before misheyakir.

- **Ross Reads It** (`/ross`): give Ross a PDF, a link or a photo of a page, and he reads it to you in your podcast app. It started as a tool for listening to a textbook chapter on a walk. Hand him a whole book and he splits it into chapters, one episode each, in a feed of its own, and if the day's allowance runs out, he picks up where he left off. He reads Hebrew too, in small doses, because the Hebrew voice costs about 40 times as much. Half an hour of audio takes about a minute, because a GPU does the reading rather than your browser. It's free for about 5 minutes a day, or 30 if you leave an email, and the whole thing is capped at a few cents a day. The name hides RSS in it, which is the part that makes it all work: RoSS.

- **Tefillin Trials: a leaderboard, and links straight to each shiur's level** (`/tefillin-trials?level=stand`, `?shiur=4`): the title screen now lists each shiur's world with its levels, so the chabura can jump straight to this week's. Every level ends with a leaderboard: type your name and your score goes up next to everyone else's.

- **Three new Parasha Books: Vayera, Chayei Sarah and Toldot** (`/parasha-books/vayera`, `/parasha-books/chayei-sarah`, `/parasha-books/toldot`): the picture books where the girls explore the weekly parasha have caught up with the calendar. From Vayera on, they get there by the Parsha Box: a little carved box on the Shabbat table that opens, grows big enough to climb into, and sets them down to watch the parasha happen in front of them, and then brings them home at the end. It's still all peshat. The kids watch Avraham run to his guests in the heat of the day, count Rivka's trips to the well for ten camels, and hear "the voice is the voice of Yaakov." Where the Torah is silent, they wonder instead of filling in a midrash. Each book has the pesukim in Hebrew and English on every page, a "look closely" question, a trailer and a printable PDF.
