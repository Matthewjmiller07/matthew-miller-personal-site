🧸 Headliner

A toy for every parsha, after Shadal

54 printable snap-together toys, one for each parsha. Each one is built on a single peshat of Shmuel David Luzzatto, with the Hebrew of his comment and a Sefaria link beside the model. Some favourites:

  • The rakia (Bereshit 1:6) is a beaten sky-blue plate holding up the upper waters, with windows the rain comes through.
  • The burning bush (Shemot 3:2): the flames stand in the gaps between the thorns and never touch them.
  • The Urim and Tumim (Vayikra 8:8) are 22 letter tiles you draw from the choshen. Alef is called “ur” and tav “tam.”
  • The Levites' open land (Bamidbar 35:4) is a stacking toy, with empty ground inside the wall as well as outside, like Rome's pomerium.
  • Ekev's clothes that didn't wear out, which Shadal reads with no miracle at all: God gave them spare clothes and shoes.

Every toy prints on a Bambu A1 mini with no supports. The page has a 3D viewer you can pull apart to see how each one goes together, plus a 3MF plate and STLs.

🐸 3D printing

The Ten Plagues, as kits

Ten snap-together kits, one per plague, on the same printer. There's a frog that has already split off a baby (Rashi's one frog), a clear hailstone that closes around a flame, and blood on the doorposts printed as a second colour.

Where the commentators disagree, you get a kit for each reading. Arov is R' Yehuda's wild animals or R' Nechemia's hornets and mosquitoes (Shemot Rabbah 11:3). Choshech is darkness over the land, or the Torah Temimah's bold suggestion (10:21) that it was a film over the pupil “the thickness of a dinar,” which you could feel with your hand. That kit is an eye with a film you can take off.

🎙️ New tool

Ross Reads It

Give Ross a PDF, a link or a photo of a page, and he reads it to you in your podcast app. It started as a way to listen to a textbook chapter on a walk. Hand him a whole book and he splits it into chapters, one episode each, in a feed of its own. If the day's allowance runs out, he picks up where he left off the next day.

He reads Hebrew too. Half an hour of audio takes about a minute, because a GPU does the reading, not your browser. It's free for about 5 minutes a day, or 30 if you leave an email. The name hides the part that makes it work: RoSS, as in RSS.

🖨️ Teardown

Taking apart a printer

I got an old HP Photosmart C6180 without a power cord, so I'm taking it apart instead. The 3D model comes apart one step at a time, in the order of the real teardown, and you can click any part to see what it does.

The surprise: there are no stepper motors in it. The carriage and the paper both run on cheap DC motors, and a clear strip and a clear disc printed with fine lines tell the printer exactly where they are. It also explains where the ink goes when the printer is just sitting there: into a felt pad.

🪬 Game · World 2

Tefillin Trials: the tefillin stand

A new world for this week's chabura (simanim 25–27: which arm, where on the arm, and when you say the bracha). You and your kid run a tefillin stand on a stone street corner. Each person who walks up has a different arm question: a lefty, someone ambidextrous, someone who writes with his left and does everything else with his right, and two soldiers wounded in the war.

Then the camera cuts to a giant arm, and you set the bayit down on the lower half of the muscle while it slides back and forth. It's scored like the shiur's basketball rule: touch the line and you're out. Then say “lehaniach” while the knot is tightening. Miss it, and the game asks what to do. (Bless anyway: every moment they're on is the mitzvah, which is why it's “lehaniach” and not “likshor.”)

Every level now ends with a leaderboard, and each shiur has its own link.

📚 For the kids

Three new Parasha Books

Vayera, Chayei Sarah and Toldot. The kids now get there by the Parsha Box, a little carved box on the Shabbat table that grows big enough to climb into and brings them home at the end. It's still all peshat: they watch Avraham run to his guests in the heat of the day, count Rivka's trips to the well for ten camels, and hear “the voice is the voice of Yaakov.” Where the Torah is silent, they wonder instead of filling in a midrash.
